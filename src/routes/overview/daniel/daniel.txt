
# Daniel: Restoration of the Kingdom — and the Key in Chapter 7

Daniel and Revelation are not history leaked in advance. They are the last chapter of a war that began in heaven.

God had two things to do. First: reveal and vindicate His **character**. The incarnation, death, and resurrection of Christ answered that. Second: vindicate His **government**. God is good. The remaining question is whether His system works. That convinced a third of the angels. These two books are about that second question.

## Government is a principle, not a place

A government is a constitution — a way of administering people — not a capital or a land mass.

Satan’s principle is self-government, sold as freedom. God says the only real freedom is the yoke of Christ. People who belong to neither America nor China nor the Vatican can still be under Satan’s government. The places are many. The principle is one.

We who read the Bible read with biased eyes. Skeptics read the same pages and come out elsewhere. That is why the judgment must be public. The universe has to see what is obvious to us — and we have a part in that vindication.

## Why Daniel matters

“None of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand” (Daniel 12:8–10).

**History:** chapters 1, 3, 4, 5, 6. **Prophecy:** 2, 7, 8, and 9–12. The history already hints at the end. Daniel 3 — the image, the command to bow — is Revelation 13 in parable: not a statue in a park, but a system of government demanding allegiance.

Daniel is not a closed account of Jewish history, fulfilled in the first century. It is preparation for the last conflict.

## Judah as a type

The Old Testament is a parable of something greater. What happened to the Jews in Daniel illustrates what happens to the church.

By then the ten tribes were gone. The book is about **Judah**, destroyed by Babylon: a people captive in a strange place. That is also the church at the end.

They mocked the messengers until there was no remedy (2 Chronicles 36). Then the sanctuary was burned, the vessels taken, the king’s eyes put out. The people remained alive only as captives.

The same history, spiritually: apostasy, men as rulers, tradition as worship, darkness. The majority of God’s people today are still His — as the Jews in Babylon were still His — but they are in captivity. We have no right to condemn them. God did not condemn us in the same dark. He came to get us out.

The exodus happens in pockets, as with Ezra and Nehemiah. A person can be in Babylon’s environment and not in Babylon’s spirit. Daniel was.

## Four things that make a kingdom — all of them gone

**Geographic** — a land mass. **Political** — constitution and administration. **Religious** — from Nimrod onward every kingdom had a cult; Judah’s was Jehovah at the sanctuary. **Demographic** — people. No people, no kingdom.

Judah lost all four. Christ’s kingdom in this age is in the same captivity: no land you can point to; most of His people under denominational politics; worship confused; the people scattered and unrecognizable. God intends to restore all four.

## Four prophecies, four restorations

Not one prophecy told four times. Four aspects of the kingdom, each vision ending at the true end — not with the Jews back in Palestine.

**Daniel 2 — geographic.** Gold, silver, bronze, iron, iron and clay: Babylon through divided Rome. Metal of the earth. It does not end at the feet. A stone strikes the image, becomes a mountain, fills the whole earth. God’s people get their country back.

**Daniel 7 — political.** Four wild beasts. Heaven’s view of earthly politics: dominance, war, succession. Lion: Babylon. Bear: Medo-Persia. Leopard: Greece. The fourth, matching no known animal: Rome — still here, wounded, re-emerging, to come up from the bottomless pit as Satan’s last kingdom on earth. The little horn: the papacy. The end: the Son of Man given an everlasting dominion. The true King on the throne.

**Daniel 8 — religious.** Ram and he-goat: sanctuary animals. Climax: 2,300 days, then the sanctuary *restored* (better than “cleansed”). Not Herod’s temple. True worship given back to the body of Christ — the sanctuary in which He dwells.

**Daniel 11–12 — demographic.** No symbols. Literal kings, north and south. End: Michael stands up; a time of trouble such as never was; “thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book.” Not a return to Palestine. Deliverance from this planet — the end of the captivity.

| Vision | Aspect | How it ends |
|---|---|---|
| Daniel 2 | Geographic | Stone becomes a mountain filling the earth |
| Daniel 7 | Political | Son of Man given everlasting dominion |
| Daniel 8 | Religious | Sanctuary restored — true worship |
| Daniel 11–12 | Demographic | The people written in the book are delivered |

## Daniel 7 is the key to Revelation

Revelation is that pre-advent judgment opened at length. (Skip the phrase “investigative judgment.” The thing itself is in the text.)

**Order in Daniel 7**

1. Four kingdoms rise. The fourth overruns the world.
2. Thrones set. Ancient of Days sits. Books opened.
3. The fourth beast is slain and given to the burning flame.
4. One like the Son of Man comes with clouds **to the Ancient of Days** — not to earth — and is given the kingdom.

This is not the white throne after the millennium. Revelation 19: the beast is thrown into the lake of fire **when Christ comes**, before the thousand years. So the court in Daniel 7 sits before that.

The other beasts lost dominion but lived on (Babylon, Persia, Greece are still on the map). The fourth, when it goes, is gone. Last beast. None after.

The clouds in 7:13 are not the second coming. Destination: the Father. Luke 19: a nobleman goes far away **to receive a kingdom, and to return**. Pentecost put the kingdom in hearts. It did not hand Him the nations. That happens at the seventh trumpet: “The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of His Christ” (Revelation 11:15).

Five kingdoms, not four. Two verdicts, both from the judgment: the beast burned; Christ given the earth forever.

He already has all authority (Matthew 28:18) as general of the war. Demons obey because they are compelled. He does not yet rule the planet. Kingdoms four and five have grown side by side for two thousand years. Then the verdict: this fifth is the first not found wanting. That is not God arbitrarily planting a throne. The whole book is successive weighing. The fifth lasts.

## Two worked examples inside Daniel

**Belshazzar.** *Mene, tekel, peres.* Numbered (to have someone’s number: an accurate reading of character), weighed in the balances, divided. Babylon measured and passed to the Medes and Persians. That phase of Satan’s kingdom had failed.

**Nebuchadnezzar.** Seven years with a beast’s heart — “by the decree of the watchers, and the demand by the word of the holy ones” (Daniel 4:17). God does not pass this sentence alone. His own kingdom is in the dock. Heaven is a jury. The twenty-four elders in Revelation hint at the same court.

## Why Christ is judged at all

Not His integrity. Not whether His people can be lost. There are other persons in the room. The charge was: *Does His way of dealing with people work?* The world still runs on educate, imprison, coerce. The 144,000 are to show that Christ in you produces the best people who have ever lived.

That has to happen **before** He comes. When He comes, the righteous are caught up. God does not need a judgment in order to know. Heaven is not run on what God knows in private. It is run on transparency. If He were the only person involved, Satan would have been gone at the first lie. He was not. That is why there is a judgment, and why we are not in heaven yet.

## Notes that keep the picture from shrinking

- **The woman is not the last enemy.** She rides the beast (papacy chief among the religious powers). The ten horns strip her — accounts, buildings, public Christianity — not literal fire. The last enemy is the beast from the pit. Those who have already left Babylon have no institutions for the state to seize; the beast will later turn on them for refusing the mark.
- **Babylon is as scattered as Israel.** God’s people are not a denomination. Neither is Babylon one city or one church.
- **Where we are.** The seventh head, for a short space; then the beast itself as the eighth, out of the bottomless pit.

Revelation is Daniel 7 opened: two kingdoms grown side by side, a court, books opened, one kingdom burned, the other given the earth forever.
