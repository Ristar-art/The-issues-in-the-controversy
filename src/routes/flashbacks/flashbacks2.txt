**Why Some Passages in Revelation Are Not in Chronological Order**

The question is simple: how can anyone say that a chapter in Revelation “belongs” earlier than where God placed it? The answer is that the *vision sequence* is not always the *event sequence*. God sometimes inserts an earlier event at a later point in the story in order to answer a question or explain a climax.

---

### The seven seals

Revelation 6 presents the seals in order:

- **Seals 1–4** — the four horses (white, red, black, pale). These stand in sequence.
- **Seal 5** — judgment begins. The souls under the altar cry for vengeance and are given white robes. White robes for the dead mean their cases have been justified. That can only happen in the judgment.
- **Seal 6** — the great day of God’s wrath arrives: earthquake, sun darkened, moon like blood, stars falling, heaven departing as a scroll, mountains and islands moved. The cry is, “the great day of his wrath is come; and who shall be able to stand?” That wrath is the seven last plagues (Revelation 15:1).
- **Seal 7** (Revelation 8) — silence in heaven, seven angels given trumpets, the censer cast down. Casting down the censer is the end of intercession: no more ministry of mercy; probation is closed.

Immediately after seal 6, **chapter 7** shows the sealing of the 144,000: do not hurt the earth, sea, or trees until God’s servants are sealed.

Sealing cannot happen *after* wrath has already begun. At that point destinies are fixed. The sealing belongs under **seal 5**, during the judgment, *before* seal 6.

God still shows it after seal 6 for a reason. The cry “Who shall be able to stand?” is part of the vision, not a transcript of real-time speech. God raises the question so He can answer it. He then shows the sealed company who *will* stand through the time of wrath. The chapter is encouragement. The sealing itself took place earlier; the *revelation* of it is placed here to answer the question.

The same pattern appears in the days of Noah: remnant sealed, then judgment falls. God often tells the story, then steps back to show what led up to it.

---

### The seven trumpets

The same principle governs the trumpets. They are understood here as the **seven last plagues**, after the close of probation—not a recap of church history from the early centuries.

In Revelation 8 the seventh seal opens, there is silence, the seven angels receive trumpets, another angel offers much incense (Day of Atonement language), then the censer is filled with fire and thrown to the earth. Only *after* that do the trumpets sound. The censer is a vessel of mercy and intercession. Throwing it down means Christ’s mediatorial work has ended. The trumpets therefore cannot be lifted out of that context and placed in AD 200–1500 (barbarians, Islam, Ottomans, and so on).

A second confirmation: Revelation 7 holds the four winds so they do not hurt the earth, sea, or trees until the sealing is finished. The trumpets then describe earth, sea, and rivers being hurt. That hurt comes *after* the sealing.

Trumpets 1–6 occupy chapters 8–9. At the end of chapter 9 one expects trumpet 7. Instead, chapters 10–11 insert three events:

1. An angel with a little open book (the book of Daniel)
2. Measuring the temple (the judgment)
3. The two witnesses who prophesy 1,260 days (the final witnessing work while the 144,000 are being made up)

Only then does trumpet 7 sound: the mystery of God is finished; the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ.

These three events do not belong *between* trumpets 6 and 7. Revelation 11:14 says the second woe (trumpet 6) is past and the third woe (trumpet 7) “cometh **quickly**.” “Quickly” rules out a long gap. The little book, the measuring of the temple, and the 1,260 days of the witnesses take time. If they sat between 6 and 7, God could not have said “quickly.” They belong under **seal 5**, during the judgment, *before* probation closes.

Why, then, are they written just before trumpet 7? Because trumpet 7 is the finishing of the mystery. The angel of chapter 10, with the open book, marks the *beginning* of the last great movement whose *end* is that finishing. When the story reaches the point at which the mystery is about to be finished, God pauses and takes the reader back: here is how we get there. Without the opened book, the measured temple, and the testifying witnesses, that climax would be unexplained.

The sixth seal (wrath), the casting of the censer, and the silence of seal 7 are essentially concurrent: end of grace, arrival of wrath.

---

### What the mystery is

It is more than “Christ in you.” Ephesians 1:9–10: God will gather together in one all things in Christ, both in heaven and on earth. The sealing of the 144,000 is the first step—Christ fully formed in a people. The mystery is not finished until the kingdoms of this world are overthrown and the universe is reconciled. People are judged; God’s government is also on trial. The final conflict shows what that government can produce. That consummation is trumpet 7.

---

### Why the traditional Adventist reading arose

If one reads Revelation as a straight timeline, the little book and the measuring of the temple sit after trumpet 6. Identifying those with 1844 forces trumpets 1–6 into the past, so interpreters searched history for hail, burning mountains, locusts, and the Euphrates.

That reading is internally inconsistent. They placed the little book and the measured temple at 1844, then jumped the two witnesses (the very next verses) back to 538–1798 and the French Revolution. Revelation 10–11 is one connected movement: eat the book, prophesy again, measure the temple, give power to the two witnesses. Yanking verse 3 a thousand years backward is done to avoid a future 1,260 days.

**Context is king.** Some 1,260-day passages (such as the woman in the wilderness in Revelation 12) belong to the past, day-for-year. This one, in this setting, is future and literal. The time period must serve the context, not the reverse. Hints in chapter 11 (Sodom and Egypt, “where also our Lord was crucified”) also point forward, not to the Dark Ages.

---

### How this is studied

The case rests on the text, sequence, and logic of Scripture—not on church authority or Ellen White. She is not treated as infallible. Adventist prophetic charts of Daniel and Revelation came largely from the pioneers (Uriah Smith and others), not from her. The standing invitation is: test it by the Bible; if it fails, reject it.

The Spirit of prophecy in Revelation is not one 19th-century writer. It is the testimony of Jesus among God’s people at the end of time.

On identity: the fellowship is not a denomination. Some still use “Seventh-day Adventist” because they keep the seventh day and look for Christ’s return. Others refuse the name because of the Trinity and legalism, while still holding pre-advent judgment, an end-time Day of Atonement, and the great controversy. The label is secondary; honesty is not. When speaking with church people, it is better to be straightforward than to appear to belong to a system one no longer holds.

### A related verse: 1 John 5:20

“The Son of God is come, and hath given us an understanding, that we may know him that is true, and we are in him that is true, even in his Son Jesus Christ. This is the true God, and eternal life.”

“Him that is true” is the Father. The Son came so we may know the Father. The word “even” is a translator’s addition. The sense is: we are in the Father by being in His Son. The true God of the verse is the One the Son came to make known. Isolated, the last clause can be misread as calling Jesus “the true God”; the whole of 1 John 5 is about Father and Son together.

---

**In short:** God sometimes places an earlier event later in the vision so that a question can be answered or a climax explained. The 144,000 are shown after seal 6 to answer “Who shall be able to stand?” Revelation 10–11 is shown before trumpet 7 to show how the mystery is finished. In both cases the *events* belong during the judgment (seal 5), before wrath and before the plagues. Once that narrative principle is seen, the apparent disorder is gone.
