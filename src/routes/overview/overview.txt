
# The Pre-Advent Judgment: A Chart of Daniel and Revelation

The message God’s people are to preach is not merely “the gospel.” It is **the gospel of the kingdom**.

John the Baptist opened with it (Matthew 3:1–2). Jesus made it the condition of the end (Matthew 24:14). When the Pharisees asked when it would appear, He said it does not come with observation: “The kingdom of God is within you” (Luke 17:20–21). Paul names what is within: “Christ in you, the hope of glory” (Colossians 1:27).

The first angel of Revelation 14 flies with “the everlasting gospel” — and Jesus has already defined that gospel as the gospel of the kingdom. The chart hangs on that.

## How to read the chart

Revelation 4–20 is a judgment between two kingdoms. Daniel 7 is the template. Revelation fills in the detail.

| Color | Time |
|---|---|
| Orange | Past |
| Green | Present — where we are now |
| Blue | Future |
| Yellow | Concluding events |

---

## I. The template: Daniel 7

Four beasts: lion, bear, leopard, terrible beast — Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome. The fourth is the last earthly kingdom. It is not replaced by another empire. It survives until Christ comes, when it is “given to the burning flame” (Daniel 7:11).

While the little horn is speaking great words and persecuting the saints, heaven opens: thrones set, the Ancient of Days seated, books opened (Daniel 7:9–10). Two verdicts follow:

1. The Son of Man is given an everlasting kingdom that all peoples shall serve (Daniel 7:13–14).
2. The beast is destroyed.

Daniel then jumps. Between the opening of the books and those two verdicts there is a blank. Revelation occupies that blank.

There are five kingdoms in Daniel 7, not four. Babylon, Medo-Persia, and Greece have already failed. Two remain: the beast, and Christ’s kingdom, which began at Pentecost. The judgment decides which is fit to rule.

---

## II. Christ’s kingdom reviewed — Revelation 5 to 11

The seven-sealed book is the chronicle of Christ’s kingdom: from Pentecost (Christ living in His people) to the seventh trumpet (the kingdoms of this world becoming His in fullness). Each seal is a chapter.

**Seals 1–4 (past)** — matching Daniel’s little horn:

- **White horse.** The kingdom begins well. The early church goes out conquering.
- **Red horse.** It begins to fall away. Peace is taken from the earth.
- **Black horse.** Apostasy. Truth is sold cheap; the kingdom courts the world.
- **Pale horse.** Worst. The rider is Death. The *visible* church becomes Satan’s instrument.

**Seal 5 (present)** — the judgment itself.

The book was opened when the judgment sat. Inside that book there is also a chapter *about* the judgment. Seal 5 is that chapter.

The souls under the altar receive white robes. They were already innocent before God. The robe now is a public clearing of their name. In the world they died as criminals; in the judgment it is seen they were righteous. This is Daniel 7’s “judgment was given to the saints”: a verdict *on their behalf*, not the saints sitting as judges.

They rest a little longer. More of their brethren will be killed. Until probation closes, Christian blood is still seed. After it closes, that purpose ends.

**This is where we are.**

**The 144,000** belong here, not after seal 6. Seal 6 already announces the day of wrath — the seven last plagues. By then God’s people must already be sealed.

They are the decisive exhibit. Seals 1–4 look like decline. If that were the whole record, Christ’s government would not have been proven. The 144,000 display the kingdom in the greatest purity yet seen. They are first fruits, without fault, the Lamb’s followers, God’s battle-axe in the contest. When they have been shown, the argument is over.

Twelve is the kingdom number. 144 is twelve twelves. The figure is almost certainly symbolic, not a headcount. They are also the group that *survives*; many other Christians die as martyrs in this period. The great multitude of Revelation 7:9 is the same company: heard as a number, then seen as a multitude. All of this occupies the same pocket of time as the mark of the beast.

**Seals 6–7 (future)** — what Daniel left blank.

Seal 6: cosmic signs; men hide and say the day of wrath has come. Seal 7: the trumpets.

At the seventh trumpet: “The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of His Christ” (Revelation 11:15). This is not Pentecost. Pentecost was the *beginning* of the kingdom. This is its *establishment*. The nations are not His yet. Daniel 7:13–14 is this same moment — not the ascension. “All people, nations, and languages should serve Him” has never been true since Pentecost.

## III. The beast’s kingdom reviewed — Revelation 12 to 19

The same four movements, from the other side.

**Past.** While the four horses run, Satan persecutes the woman through the beast (Revelation 12–13): Nero, the Dark Ages, the dragon cast down.

**Present.** A beast rises from the earth: America as a world power, during the judgment. Then the mark of the beast — the perfecting of Satan’s kingdom, running in parallel with the 144,000. We are in this zone now, moving into that crisis.

**Future.** The earth is harvested; the grapes go into the winepress (Revelation 14). Then the seven last plagues, matching the trumpets.

**End.** The beast is cast into the lake of fire (Revelation 19). That is Daniel’s second verdict, matching the moment Christ receives the kingdom.

## The chart, read across

| | **Daniel 7** | **Christ’s kingdom (Rev 5–11)** | **Beast’s kingdom (Rev 12–19)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Past** | Little horn persecutes | Seals 1–4: four horses | Dragon persecutes the woman |
| **Present** | Judgment sits; books opened | Seal 5; 144,000 perfected | America rises; mark of the beast |
| **Future** | *(blank in Daniel)* | Seal 6 cosmic signs; trumpets | Harvest; seven last plagues |
| **End** | Christ given dominion; beast burned | Seventh trumpet | Beast into the lake of fire |

Details still to place on this grid — the two witnesses, the little book, the woman on the beast — fit inside it. They do not replace it.

Chapters 1–3 are introduction. Chapters 21–22 are epilogue. The main body is the contest of the two kingdoms.

---

## Notes that keep the picture from blurring

- **Not a salvation test.** Millions died in Christ without understanding Revelation. The book is for the last generation who finish the work of the kingdom — privilege, not a ticket into heaven.
- **Martyrs during the sealing** still win people, because probation is not closed. After it closes, that purpose ends.
- **Default reading: symbolic.** The unusual case is the literal one. Locusts with lions’ teeth are not zoology. The drying Euphrates is not the physical river; the sixth plague comes *after* the mark of the beast. Those who read it as a land-bridge for an army against literal Israel have already mistaken who God’s people are.
- **Why it is hard.** It was not designed for the careless, or for Satan. Truth is revealed, not discovered, and it is revealed to the submissive. Dullness of mind is not a disqualification. Dullness of spirit is.
